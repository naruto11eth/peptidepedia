## What it is

NAD+ is a coenzyme present in every cell, central to converting food into ATP and required by the sirtuins and PARP enzymes involved in DNA repair. Levels decline with age. That decline is real and well documented, and it is the entire basis of the NAD+ supplement industry.

## The evidence asymmetry

This is the cleanest example on the site of a claim outrunning its evidence:

| Form | Randomised controlled trials in humans |
| --- | --- |
| Oral NMN | 12+ |
| Oral NR | 10+ |
| Injectable NAD+ (SC or IV) | 0 |

The oral precursor trials consistently show one thing: **they raise blood NAD+ levels safely**. What they largely have not shown is that raising NAD+ produces the outcomes people want - more energy, better cognition, slower ageing. Even the well-studied form has a mechanism-to-outcome gap.

Injectable NAD+ has a genuine pharmacokinetic argument in its favour: it bypasses the gut and the enzymatic conversion steps that vary between individuals, so bioavailability is unambiguously higher. **Higher bioavailability of a molecule whose clinical benefit is unproven is not the same as a better outcome**, and there is no trial comparing the routes on anything that matters.

## Practical notes

- **The injection hurts.** This is not a sourcing problem or a technique problem; it is characteristic of the molecule. Diluting further and injecting slowly helps.
- **Rate is everything for IV.** Flushing, nausea and chest tightness during infusion are rate-dependent and resolve on slowing down.
- **Subcutaneous is more sustainable than IV** for ongoing use - cheaper, done at home, no clinic time - which is why most people who continue past the novelty phase end up there.
- **NAD+ degrades.** Discolouration or precipitate means discard, not push through.

## On sourcing

NAD+ is a case where batch quality varies more than usual, and vendors that test every batch and pull failures are behaving correctly rather than unusually. A supplier telling you a batch failed and is out of stock is giving you the most useful signal a supplier can give. See [safety and sourcing](/safety).
