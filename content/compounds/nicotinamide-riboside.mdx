## What it is

Nicotinamide riboside is a form of vitamin B3 that enters the NAD salvage pathway one step further out than [NMN](/compounds/nmn). Both raise NAD. The commercial argument between them has been fierce and the evidence separating them is thin.

NR's distinguishing feature is regulatory rather than biochemical: it secured GRAS status and dietary ingredient notifications early, so it has been continuously and lawfully sold while NMN was banned, unbanned and litigated over.

## What the trials show

NR has more completed randomised human trials than anything else in this category, which is the strongest thing that can be said for it.

They consistently show that oral NR raises blood NAD in a dose-dependent way and is well tolerated over months. Beyond that the record is mixed to disappointing. Trials in cardiovascular function, insulin sensitivity, muscle performance, Parkinson's disease and cognitive impairment have produced small, inconsistent, or null results. A recent phase 2 randomised pilot in older adults with amnestic mild cognitive impairment adds to a pattern of studies that establish the biomarker moves and struggle to show that anything else does.

That pattern is the honest summary of the whole NAD precursor category. The pharmacokinetics work. The clinical benefit has not been demonstrated.

## Practical notes

- **The NR versus NMN argument is largely commercial.** Both raise NAD. No adequately powered head to head trial has shown either is superior in humans, and the loudest claims come from parties with a stake in one of them.
- **Well tolerated is a real finding.** Against most of this site, a compound with multiple randomised trials showing good tolerability over months is a relatively comfortable position, even without efficacy.
- **Judge it as a supplement, not a therapy.** It raises a biomarker safely at a meaningful price. Whether that is worth buying is a different question from whether it is safe.
